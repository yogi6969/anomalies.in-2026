SAP has extended the Compatibility Pack deadline to May 31, 2026 — one final window for enterprises to begin their S/4HANA transition on stable footing. After that date, new Compatibility Pack releases stop. ECC mainstream maintenance ends December 31, 2027.

The uncomfortable reality: more than 60% of SAP customers have not yet migrated.

For Indian enterprises running mission-critical operations on SAP ECC — manufacturing lines, procurement systems, financial consolidation, statutory reporting — the gap between where most organisations are and where they need to be is closing faster than most IT roadmaps reflect.

## The Timeline Is Tighter Than You Think

The default assumption is that December 2027 is the relevant endpoint. It is not. The real constraint is migration duration.

Realistic SAP S/4HANA migrations — the kind that preserve business continuity and handle custom code remediation properly — take 18 to 36 months. An organisation that begins in mid-2026 is targeting a go-live in late 2027 or 2028. At that point, they are either cutting across the support deadline or rushing the final phases to beat it.

The cost dimension sharpens the urgency further. Consulting rates for qualified S/4HANA talent are already elevated. As the December 2027 deadline concentrates demand — with a significant portion of that unmigrated 60% attempting to execute simultaneously — rates are projected to rise 10 to 20% through 2027. Organisations that begin partner selection now will secure better terms and more experienced teams than those entering the market in twelve months.

The window for an orderly migration at reasonable cost is open. It will not remain open indefinitely.

## Why Indian Enterprises Are Especially Exposed

India's enterprise SAP footprint is concentrated in sectors where a botched migration carries serious operational consequences: manufacturing, pharmaceuticals, logistics, and public sector infrastructure. These environments cannot absorb system outages or data integrity failures quietly.

The additional complication is custom ABAP code. Many large Indian enterprises built substantial customisations on ECC over two decades — workflows, regulatory reports, and business logic that does not migrate automatically. It must be assessed against S/4HANA's simplified data model, rewritten where underlying tables no longer exist, and tested comprehensively. For organisations with large ABAP estates, this remediation phase is frequently the longest and the most underestimated.

Indian subsidiaries of global enterprises face a parallel problem. The India entity — often running localised configurations for GST compliance, TDS, and statutory reporting — typically sits on a separate ECC instance with its own customisation history. The India migration cannot simply follow the global template.

## What This Means for Enterprise Technology Leaders

For CIOs and VP-level technology leaders, the S/4HANA deadline is not primarily a technical problem. It is a budget and resource planning problem that has a technical solution.

The board conversation needs reframing. The relevant comparison is not "migration cost vs. doing nothing." It is "migration cost now vs. migration cost in 2027 at 15% higher rates, with less experienced partners, under time pressure, with reduced negotiating leverage." That comparison is more compelling than most organisations are currently making.

Vendor selection is urgent. The best SAP S/4HANA implementation partners in India are already booking 2026 and early 2027 capacity. Organisations that delay RFP and selection until the second half of 2026 will find their options are narrower than expected.

There is also the question of operational continuity during a migration running two or more years. While your SAP migration runs, [ERP integration automation](/solutions/erp-integration/) can bridge the gap — pulling real-time data from both legacy ECC and new S/4HANA instances, keeping downstream reporting and connected workflows functional throughout the transition rather than waiting for a hard cutover.

## What to Do About It

**1. Commission a custom code impact assessment now.** SAP's Readiness Check and Custom Code Migration App can generate this analysis in four to eight weeks. This single deliverable will determine whether your migration is an 18-month programme or a 30-month one.

**2. Lock in an implementation partner in the next 90 days.** Issue the RFP now. The talent market is tightening and waiting until late 2026 is a decision to accept either reduced partner quality or significantly higher costs.

**3. Run automation investment in parallel with migration.** The migration will take years. Operational improvement cannot wait. Build your integration and automation layer against current ECC systems in a way that extends cleanly to S/4HANA — do not pause transformation work waiting for a cutover that is 24 months away.

**4. Get board visibility on delay costs.** Technology leaders encountering funding resistance need to put a number on the cost of waiting — higher rates, compressed timelines, reduced partner choice, and extended support risk exposure. The case for acting in 2026 is quantifiable.

Get a [free automation blueprint](/blueprint/) to identify which ERP processes to automate first — and which can be stabilised now to reduce operational risk through the migration period.

The May 2026 deadline is three months away. The December 2027 deadline is 21 months away. Neither number is as comfortable as it looks when measured against a migration clock that starts at 18 months.
