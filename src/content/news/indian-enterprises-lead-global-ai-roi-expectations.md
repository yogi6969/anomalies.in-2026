India is not following the global AI adoption curve. It is ahead of it.

According to SAP's Value of AI Report 2025, 93% of Indian businesses expect a positive return on their AI investments within three years — the highest rate of any country surveyed globally. The average expected ROI stands at 15% in 2025, with respondents projecting that figure will more than double to 31% within two years.

These are not vanity numbers. Indian companies are backing these expectations with real capital: the average AI investment stands at $31 million, above the global average of $26.7 million. This is a market that has moved from experimentation to conviction.

## From Pilot to Production

The SAP data aligns with findings from EY and the Confederation of Indian Industry. Their AIdea of India report found that 47% of Indian enterprises already have multiple Generative AI use cases running in production — not in testing, not in planning, but live and operational.

This is a meaningful benchmark. It means nearly half of Indian enterprises have crossed the hardest threshold in AI adoption: getting past the pilot stage and into systems that are actually being used to run business operations.

The use cases that have reached production share common characteristics. They are process-specific, they have clear success metrics, and they were scoped tightly enough to deliver results within a quarter or two. Broad AI transformation programmes with 18-month timelines are not what is crossing the production threshold. Focused, outcome-linked implementations are.

## What This Means for VP and AVP Technology Leaders

If you are a VP or AVP of Technology, Finance, or Operations at an Indian enterprise, the data creates both an opportunity and a pressure.

The opportunity: your organisation likely has multiple high-value targets for AI automation where the ROI case is already proven elsewhere. Invoice processing, procurement reconciliation, vendor communication, and approval workflows are running on AI pipelines at comparable organisations right now. The question is not whether the technology works — it does. The question is how quickly you can implement it in your specific environment.

The pressure: your peers are moving. 47% of enterprises already have multiple GenAI use cases in production. Enterprise technology leaders who are still in pilot mode at the end of 2026 will be explaining a capability gap, not a cautious strategy.

The practical path forward is not a large-scale AI transformation programme. It is a scoped 90-day implementation that targets a single high-volume, high-pain process — one where the inputs are defined, the success criteria are measurable, and the workflow can be automated without requiring changes to your core ERP or finance systems.

For most Indian enterprises, the highest-return starting point is finance operations. [Invoice automation](/solutions/invoice-automation/) alone — extracting data from multi-format vendor invoices and running automated 3-way matching — can reduce month-end close cycles from 15 days to 2 days. That is the kind of result that shows up in the CFO's next board presentation.

For organisations ready to go further, [agentic workflow automation](/solutions/agentic-workflows/) applies the same logic to multi-step processes: procurement approvals, vendor onboarding, exception routing, and escalation management.

## The Execution Gap

The risk embedded in the SAP data is not that Indian enterprises are being too aggressive. The risk is that high expectations create pressure for visible progress, which can lead to broad programme launches that are too complex to deliver.

The 93% who expect positive ROI within three years will only achieve it if they choose implementation approaches that can actually produce results in that window. Scoped pilots with defined success criteria, 90-day delivery windows, and outcome-linked engagement structures are the mechanism that separates the 47% already in production from the majority still planning.

The data is clear. The market is moving. The question for enterprise technology leaders is not whether to invest in AI — it is whether to invest in ways that deliver measurable outcomes within a defined timeframe.
